The Decision-Information Dilution Hypothesis & Unified Beta–KL Architecture

When most clinical arrivals have baseline risks well below τ* = CFP/(CFP + CFN), subthreshold decoys can dominate global conditional mutual information rankings despite rarely moving posterior probabilities across τ*. Coordinating valuation, pilot sampling, and residual specialization via one shared Beta–KL threshold kernel recovers decision-relevant tie-breakers under decoy competition while matching global learning when single biomarkers act homogeneously.

1. Shared Beta–KL Threshold Identity

Building on Buja, Stuetzle & Shen (2005)'s mode-aligned Beta(1+κτ*, 1+κ(1−τ*)) proper score, dividing wτ*,κ(p) by its peak at τ* equals the Boltzmann Bernoulli KL gate identically (< 6 × 10−15), extracting higher signal-to-noise than discontinuous fixed-threshold utility in finite pilots.

wτ*,κ(p)/w(τ*) = exp(−κ DKL(Bern(τ*) || Bern(p)))

2. Two-Phase Stratified Pilot Discovery

Constrained Neyman allocation concentrates Phase-2 pilots on High-Signal False Positives and boundary cases using linear-weight Horvitz–Thompson variance (wiri2 in V̂I, reducing identically to s2/n under SRS), pairing disjoint 50/50 split-pilot confirmation with high-power 4-fold cross-fitted discovery.

V̂total = [n/(n−1)] N−2 Σ wiri2 + N−2 Σ Nh2(1−nh/Nh)sh2/nh

3. KL-Gated Residual & Panel-Cost Ledger

Combines a 100%-sample unweighted global LightGBM backbone with an OOF-stacked KL-gated residual specialist (monitored via scale-invariant RMS contribution RCZ), locking a common panel Scommon across ensemble members so specimen collection and CPT assay fees are billed coherently.

Ci(Ai) = I{Ai ≠ ∅} cproc(Ai) + Σm∈Ai cassay,m
