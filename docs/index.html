Why Rare-Event Clinical Models Lose Diagnostic Signal — and Three Predicates That Rescue It

In emergency medicine and ICU triage, 90%–98% of arrivals are routine discharges far below the clinical action threshold τ*. Standard global conditional mutual information (log-loss) devotes unbounded curvature near p → 0, while naive row downweighting collapses effective sample size and starves decision-tree leaves.

1. Eliminate the True-Negative Singularity

By Schervish's integral representation, log-loss weights decision thresholds by wlog(τ) = [τ(1−τ)]−1 → ∞ as τ → 0+, letting subthreshold decoys outscore bedside tie-breakers. Replacing wlog with a Beta(1+κτ*, 1+κ(1−τ*)) kernel yields a closed-form strictly proper score peaking at τ* with zero weight at 0 and 1.

S(y,p) = −y[β/(α+β)][1−Ip(α,β+1)] − (1−y)[α/(α+β)]Ip(α+1,β)

2. Target High-Signal False Positives in Pilots

Beyond rare True Positives, the most informative historical controls are High-Signal False Positives — non-events whose baseline triage risk collides with True Positives near τ* (e.g., pain-driven tachycardia mimicking sepsis). Constrained Neyman allocation concentrates two-phase pilot budgets in this collision zone with exact Horvitz–Thompson two-phase variance and Holm LCB stopping.

σ̃h,CZ = wτ*,κ(p̄h) · p̄h(1−p̄h)(|yh−p̄h| + 0.15) + 10−3

3. Modulate Residuals Without Leaf Starvation

Instead of discarding 90%+ of training rows, FluidCollisionBackboneClassifier keeps a 100%-sample unweighted global Stage-2 backbone and adds a bottlenecked collision specialist modulated by a smooth probabilistic-OR Bernoulli KL gate under canonical Bernoulli MLE. Starved leaves shrink unconditionally back to the global Stage-2 backbone rather than zero.

f(x,z) = α0 + βglob fglob(x,z) + βCZ πunion(x,z) ΔCZ(ℓ1, fglob, zS)
